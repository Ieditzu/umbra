# City coverage

Umbra calculates walking routes, building shadows, 3D buildings and building measurements from prepared OpenStreetMap (OSM) snapshots. The generation pipeline targets **București and all county seats in Romania**. Since 29 October 2026 the checked-in index contains all of them: 41 cities in 49 areas, with București split into 9 zones (table below). No city is named in the server code: the server reads `mobile/assets/data/cities/index.json` and chooses the available area by position. A city becomes routable only after its files and index entry are generated and published.

Elsewhere in Romania, the server shows building outlines and approximate shadows from OpenFreeMap vector tiles, as before. These areas have no walking network, so routes there say that the points are outside the prepared walking data. They do not name any city.

## Selection

`scripts/discover-cities.py` reads the national OSM extract and does the following:

1. It takes every county boundary tagged `ISO3166-2=RO-*`.
2. It reads each county's `admin_centre` node. Ilfov lists București as its centre, so seats are deduplicated by OSM node and București is counted once. This gives **41 cities**: București and the other 40 county seats.
3. It finds the municipal boundary relation (`admin_level=8`; county level for București) that names the seat and contains its node.
4. It orders the cities by the OSM `population` tag. These tags mostly carry the 2011 census figure and are used only for ordering.

Each city's routing bounds are the bounding box of its municipal boundary. The box can include villages, forests and fields that belong to the municipality. Buildings and paths keep a margin of about 1 km, plus the existing 500 m shadow buffer.

București is split into a **3×3 grid of zones** that overlap by roughly 1 km. The server loads only the zones in use. A route between two neighbouring zones, including diagonal neighbours, joins the two graphs through their shared OSM node ids. Routes that span more than two zones are refused, with a request to choose a closer destination.

Constanța keeps its earlier snapshot: the 2026-10-25 extract with its satellite canopy layer, the walking graph built under the earlier pilot policy, and the data used in the field study and the paper. Suceava keeps its files and building ids, but its walking graph was rebuilt from the same 2026-10-28 extract under the extended street policy below. Its walking graph went from 34,070 to 42,498 nodes; its 34,056 buildings and Copernicus heights are unchanged. Both cities are listed in the same index.

## Walking network

The importer previously used only `footway`, `path`, `steps`, `pedestrian`, `residential`, `service` and `living_street` ways. Where sidewalks along main streets are not mapped separately, that splits a city into islands. In București's central zone, only 42% of walking nodes were in the largest connected component, and a 3 km route between zones failed. Public `unclassified`, `tertiary`, `secondary` and `primary` streets are now also used, unless tagged `sidewalk=separate`, `no` or `none`. The centreline stands in for an unmapped sidewalk, so exposure on these segments is always *unknown*. With this change the largest component holds 92% of the zone's nodes. `trunk` and `motorway` ways are used only with `foot=yes` or `designated`. See [the model notes](backend/model.md#pedestrian-graph).

## Sources

- **OSM:** Geofabrik Romania extract `romania-261028.osm.pbf`, replication timestamp `2026-10-28T21:21:02Z`, downloaded 2026-10-29 from https://download.geofabrik.de/europe/romania.html (MD5 verified). © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0. Each manifest records the source and packed/raw SHA-256 checksums.
- **Building heights (satellite estimates):** Copernicus Land Monitoring Service, Urban Atlas Building Block Height 2012 (10 m raster). The pipeline downloads each city's prepackaged file through the CLMS API. The newer 2021 product is requested only with `--with-2021`, because its custom-area jobs take a long time to process. © European Union, Copernicus Land Monitoring Service 2022, European Environment Agency (EEA).
- **Trees:** the existing Romania-wide OSM tree snapshot (`romania-trees.json`). Satellite canopy estimates exist only in the Constanța layer.

Copernicus values fill only buildings that have no OSM `height`, `building:levels` or `min_height` tag. A building receives a value only from raster pixels that fall entirely inside its own footprint, using the median of those pixels. In the app these values are marked **satellite estimates** with their source year. They use a heuristic range of −5/+10 m, which is not a validated confidence interval. Shade from them counts as *estimated* with reduced routing weight. OSM tags and confirmed user measurements take priority. Buildings that are still without a height stay *unknown*: they are never treated as sun or as shade.

Six county seats have no Urban Atlas building-height file: Reșița, Zalău, Vaslui, Slobozia, Alexandria and Miercurea Ciuc. They use OSM heights and floor counts only, and buildings without either stay unknown.

The comparison column is a consistency check against OSM `height` tags, which are not surveyed ground truth. Many samples are tiny: n = 1 in Bacău and Deva, n = 3 in Sfântu Gheorghe. In Iași the estimates agree poorly with floor counts (19% within the 2.5–3.5 m per floor range, against 55–61% in Cluj-Napoca, Timișoara and Bacău). Treat these values as rough.

Several cities have few buildings mapped in OSM. Slobozia has 2,085, Zalău 2,252, Călărași 2,281, Reșița 2,481, Vaslui 3,152, Focșani 3,209 and Tulcea 3,264. There, shadows and routes miss most real obstacles, and exposure without a mapped obstacle stays *no identified shade* or *unknown*, never confirmed sun.

## Server memory and startup

The server no longer decodes cities at startup. Each worker becomes ready after loading the tree catalogue, and only then decodes a city on its first request in that city. Each worker keeps a small LRU cache of decoded areas, set by `UMBRA_CITY_CACHE` (default 3). A joined pair of București zones counts as one area.

Measured on 2026-10-29 on this 8-core PC (Node 22, 15 GB RAM), with the real HTTP server and two workers. RSS covers the whole process:

| | Before (Constanța + Suceava preloaded) | Now (on demand) |
|---|---|---|
| Server ready | 5.2 s, 1,153 MB | 0.7 s, 308 MB |
| First request in a city (load + answer) | – | 1.7 s (Suceava) to 5.2 s (central București zone) |
| Later building requests | – | 6–110 ms |
| Route across two București zones | not possible | 11–12.6 s first time (two zones joined), 2.9 s after |
| After visiting 6 cities and one zone pair | – | about 1.6 GB |

Each decoded city adds about 150–400 MB, depending on its size. The worst case is two workers each holding three large areas, roughly 2.5–3 GB; lower `UMBRA_CITY_CACHE` on machines with less memory. The request deadline is 20 s, and the first cross-zone route uses most of it on this PC; a slower PC can time out on that first request and succeed on retry. Phone performance was not measured.

## Limitations

- OSM completeness varies between cities. Many buildings have no height. Paths, crossings and access tags can be missing or wrong. None of the cities has been checked in the field; the field study took place in Constanța only.
- Urban Atlas 2012 imagery is more than a decade old. Buildings can have been demolished, built or changed since then.
- Routing bounds are rectangles. Points in a neighbouring municipality that falls inside the rectangle can be routed, while suburbs outside every rectangle (for example in Ilfov) cannot.
- Two București zones can be joined for one route. Longer routes across the city are refused, and a route between diagonal zones can miss paths through the two zones that are not loaded.
- Street centrelines used as walking links have unknown exposure, and their sidewalks or crossings are not verified.
- Population figures come from OSM tags (mostly the 2011 census) and only set the build order.

## Rebuild

```bash
# 1. Download the newest Romania extract (about 330 MB) outside Git.
curl -L -o ~/.cache/umbra-server/osm/romania-latest.osm.pbf \
  https://download.geofabrik.de/europe/romania-latest.osm.pbf
# 2. Put the CLMS service key in .umbra-data/copernicus/service-key.json (ignored by Git).
# 3. Build every city, or a few with --only cluj-napoca,iasi.
uv run --python 3.12 --with osmium==4.3.0 --with rasterio --with pyproj --with 'pyjwt[crypto]' \
  python -I scripts/build-cities.py ~/.cache/umbra-server/osm/romania-YYMMDD.osm.pbf
```

The build takes about an hour on an 8-core PC. Existing outputs are kept unless you pass `--force`. Rerunning resumes pending Copernicus jobs (`.umbra-data/copernicus/tasks.json`). Review the manifests and `git diff --stat` before committing new snapshots.
