UMBRA
Shadow-based inference of occluded approach for fixed cameras

STATUS NOTE: This file is the research design. README.md and
docs/competition/ describe the current software. The estimator is
heuristic. Lead time is measured on the main camera (shadow onset vs
the moment a person enters the frame). It is not Bouman-style
non-line-of-sight reconstruction and does not invert sun/corner
geometry.

1. The idea in one sentence

Umbra watches the pavement beside a building corner. When someone
approaches from the hidden side, their shadow can reach that pavement
before their body does. Umbra investigates whether changes in this
indirect visual evidence support a reliable warning of an occluded
approach, under sunlight and artificial illumination. It measures the
warning gained, the false alarms introduced, and when an estimate
should be withheld.

It is not a camera that sees through walls. It is not the faint
penumbra method from Bouman et al. 2017. It uses a normal, visible
cast shadow as a precursor to line-of-sight.

2. What Umbra is trying to solve

At a blind corner a camera (or a driver, or a pedestrian) only reacts
after the other person appears. The practical question is:

Under which lighting, scene geometry, and camera conditions can a
fixed RGB camera infer an occluded approach from a visible cast
shadow, with useful lead time at a controlled false-alert rate?

The research contribution to investigate is a measured operating
range: when indirect shadow evidence is informative, how uncertainty
changes as conditions vary, and how well a method transfers to an
unseen corner. The intended CCTV application is early approach
warning. Shadow evidence alone does not establish criminal intent
or demonstrate robbery prevention.

3. Core principle

Sunlight (or another strong directional light) throws a hard shadow of
the hidden walker onto the visible ground near the edge. That shadow
is ordinary photometry: dark pixels on pavement. It is not a 0.1%
penumbra.

Sequence in one continuous main-camera clip:

1. Empty pavement (matches the calibration).
2. Shadow creeps in / grows toward the corner. The walker is still
   out of frame.
3. The walker steps into view.

Umbra timestamps (2) and (3). Lead time is t_line_of_sight minus
t_shadow. A useful system warns in (2) and does not invent a person
when the corner stays empty.

4. System design

Inputs

- A fixed RGB camera with a wide, clear view of the vertical corner
  edge, the visible pavement where the shadow will arrive, and the
  full body of the walker after they clear the edge. This is a route
  view, not a close facial shot.
- A short empty-scene calibration video (same lock-off, nobody
  approaching).
- Optional second camera on the hidden path for independent labels
  (ArUco 10 near the corner, 11 far). If markers are unavailable,
  the local dashboard can save a manual review: click a corner-side
  reference point, a far reference point, and the walker in selected
  frames. Not used for the warning.

Processing

1. Detect the corner edge and a pavement region of interest. If that
   selection misses the shadow path, a reviewer can mark the pavement
   on the phone clip. The first 1.8 seconds of that clip then form a
   scene-specific empty baseline for the selected patch.
2. Build a background from the empty calibration.
3. On the trial clip, measure darkening in the pavement ROI (shadow)
   and anonymous foreground residual in a broad entry zone above it
   (the full body becoming visible).
4. Track a coarse 1-D shadow position along the pavement.
   If the shadow travels clearly before the body enters view, show a
   schematic relative-motion estimate in the corner-trial video. A
   nearby static wall edge can be estimated from the empty calibration;
   In the recorded Corner trial, show a green dot moving inside the
   automatically inferred building-side route to represent estimated
   hidden position. Drive its position from measured shadow movement; do
   not animate a gait. The live camera should remain a clean preview
   until a shadow-to-hidden-path mapping is calibrated and checked. Do
   not draw a placeholder person or a raw dark-pixel rectangle over the
   live feed. Shadow alone does not support reliable limb pose or body
   shape. Keep the camera fixed after calibration. No hidden-route points
   are required from the user.
5. Gate on calibration quality, shadow strength, and temporal
   coherence. Refuse when the scene or signal is unusable.
   Use the scene's recording date, time, and coordinates to calculate
   sun elevation and azimuth. When the sun is below the horizon, accept
   a reviewed streetlamp-lit pavement area instead of a solar-shadow
   region, provided the empty calibration shows adequate stable light.
   Withhold the warning at night if that area is missing or too dark.
   The live scene clock shows the sun position for its displayed time,
   including a frozen/editable time. Sun bearing or lamp illumination
   alone cannot locate a person behind a wall without calibrated camera
   and ground geometry.
6. For controlled evaluation, use ArUco labels when available or
   clearly label a markerless truth video as operator-reviewed. A
   reviewer can also mark the first full-body entry frame in the main
   video; that timestamp is reported as manual, not automatic.

Outputs

- no coverage / no reliable signal / approaching (shadow only) /
  early warning (shadow then person) / entered view (person with no
  prior shadow).
- Shadow onset time, line-of-sight onset time, lead time in seconds.
- Coarse shadow position range, approach direction, speed class.
- Confidence and explicit refusal reasons.
- A replayable analysis video: corner edge, pavement shadow region,
  anonymous entry zone, darkening pixels, signal scores, and the
  detected shadow/body events drawn over the main footage. A small
  shadow-derived motion overlay appears only for qualifying pre-entry
  shadow travel; it is not a behind-wall body detection.

5. Research question

How much warning can visible shadow evidence provide about an
occluded approach at a fixed camera, and can that warning be retained
across unseen corners, lighting conditions, and camera noise without
an unacceptable increase in false alerts?

Hypotheses to evaluate

H1: When the geometry exposes a shadow before the walker, pavement
tracking provides positive warning time relative to direct visibility.
H2: Temporal coherence and refusal gates reduce false alerts from
lighting changes compared with a fixed darkening threshold, at a
matched detection rate.
H3: Calibration quality and lighting conditions explain changes in
performance on unseen scenes and later recording sessions.
H4: A stable streetlamp can provide warning evidence at night, but
multiple lights, flicker, and moving headlights alter the reliability
of that evidence.
H5: Performance changes across recording locations and sessions;
the size and causes of this variation can be measured.

These are research hypotheses, not established results. The full
protocol, proposed comparisons, and reporting requirements are in
docs/research-plan.md.

6. What this is not

- Not "seeing around corners" from penumbra (Bouman 2017). Cite that
  paper as related work and as a stricter, harder method. Umbra's
  contribution is a measurable early-warning protocol on a visible
  shadow.
- Not identity, pose, or through-wall imaging.
- Not a trained detector that you feed random clips. Analysis is
  per-trial residual comparison. A reviewed phone pavement region
  tunes one trial's signal; it is not evidence of generalization.
  The optional PyTorch net remains synthetic/advisory.

7. Controlled experiment

Camera 1 (main): full route view — visible pavement + edge + shadow
path + enough of the opening to show the walker's full body after
entry. Tape it down. Same pose for empty and trial.

Camera 2 (optional ground truth): hidden path, markers 10 and 11.
When you cannot use markers, film the whole route stably; in Umbra's
Manual labels panel, click the near/far route endpoints once and the
walker's feet in several paused frames. These become manual labels,
not model predictions.

For every trial

- Empty calibration, 8–15 s, nobody on either side.
- One continuous main clip: walker starts fully hidden, shadow should
  appear first, then their full body enters the frame.
- Sun must throw the shadow toward the main camera. If the shadow
  falls behind the walker, pick another corner or time of day.
- Empty-target trials (nobody comes) for false alerts.

Compare

1. Frame/background differencing on the whole image.
2. A fixed shadow-energy threshold.
3. Coverage/coherence without a shadow tracker.
4. Umbra: scene-normalized pavement residual, shadow tracker,
   line-of-sight band, lead time, confidence gate.

Measure

- Shadow-onset precision/recall vs empty trials.
- Lead time (seconds) when the person does enter.
- Fraction of approaches with lead time > 0.
- False-alert rate on empty scenes.
- Correct-refusal rate when there is no usable edge or light.

8. Honest limits

Needs directional light and a geometry where the shadow hits the
visible pavement before the body does. Fails in overcast light, when
the sun is behind the walker, on busy/reflective ground, or if the
camera moves. A hard shadow in the main camera is line-of-sight
photometry of the shadow, not reconstruction of a hidden scene.

The full-body view is used only to make the entry timestamp and the
shadow-to-body comparison reviewable. Umbra does not identify the
walker, infer pose, or reconstruct appearance; keep the camera a
wide route view rather than a close facial shot.

9. Technology

Local laptop. Python, OpenCV, FastAPI, optional PyTorch. Browser
dashboard for import, recording, analysis, and evidence export. No
cloud.

10. Starting literature (related, not implemented)

Bouman et al., "Turning Corners Into Cameras," ICCV 2017 — penumbra
NLOS; Umbra does not run that inverse problem.

The project investigates the relationship between occlusion, visible
shadow evidence, warning time, and false alarms. Its value depends on
controlled comparisons and independently reviewed results.
